Skyward Sword is more linear in its structure than the other games.

Breath of the Wild and Tears of the Kingdom are much more open; you get all your major abilities at the beginning of the game, and pretty much the whole world open to you. Environmental barriers are overcome with purchasable/findable clothing, special foods, and stamina upgrades unlocked through exploration.

## Metroidvanias

These differ from Zelda-like games in that backtracking through the interconnected world is often a requirement for progression. In Zelda-like games, the game world is more frequently an overworld hub with dungeon spokes, and revisited old locations generally isn't a progression requirement, though you can often get optional upgrades.

There are a couple types: there are games where player power is entirely determined by exploration and pickups, and games where defeating enemies rewards experience, and the player character can become more powerful independent of selected equipment.
